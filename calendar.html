Sets, cardinality, and Cartesian products; defining the objects being counted; the sum rule; importance of checking disjointness.

Wed., Aug. 19

Product rule; strings as central counting problems; strings with and without repetition Counting by complement - the subtraction rule; uniform overcounting - the division rule; factorials and permutations;

LN: Set notation and basic counting: from section The product rule to the end.
LN: More counting techniques: from the start through the factorial and -permutation formula in Unordered selections: combinations without repetition.

3

Fri., Aug. 21

Example of incorrect use of the subtraction principle: number of pins with at least one even number and no repeated digits. Review: permutation. Alternative interpretation: as an application of the division principle. The factorial; the binomial coefficient. Combinations as unordered selections. Modeling common combinatorial problems: subsets of fixed number of elements; all subsets of a set; committees of k line members; commitees with distinguished roles (chair, vice chair, etc.), functions from A to B, injective functions from A to B. Paths on the integer lattice from (0,0) to (a,b) going only right or up.

LN: Set notation and basic counting: review The product rule.
LN: More counting techniques: from the start through Counting problems.
