      Midterm 2 Information#

      Format and rules#

      Midterm 2 lasts 50 minutes and has five problems.

      You may bring one handwritten study sheet or formula sheet on one A4 page, written on both sides. No other notes or outside materials may be used during the exam. You are encouraged to use an offline calculator.

      Show the method that justifies each answer. A numerical answer or a closed formula without supporting work may receive only partial credit. When you solve a recurrence, state the characteristic polynomial, the form of the general solution, and how you used the initial conditions.

      Coverage#

      The exam covers rook polynomials, generating functions, and recurrence relations: the material from Lecture 12 (rook polynomials) through the end of the unit on finite state machines and vector recurrence relations. It corresponds to Lecture Notes in Unit 2 (generating functions) and Unit 3 (recurrence relations), together with the notes on complex numbers and on finite state machines. Material from Unit 1 is not tested directly, but you will still use binomial coefficients, counting arguments, and algebra.

      Topics to practice#

      You should be able to model a counting problem by a generating function or a recurrence and then compute with it: extract coefficients, find closed formulas, and check them on small cases. In particular, practice solving problems of the following kinds.

      • Rook polynomials

        • Compute rook numbers and the rook polynomial of a complete rectangular board.

        • Compute rook numbers of a small board of allowed squares by listing placements.

        • Use the product rule for a board that splits into pieces with disjoint rows and disjoint columns.

        • Use the use-or-omit rule for a chosen square to reduce a board to smaller boards.

      • Writing generating functions

        • Write the generating function for choices of objects where the exponent records the number of objects chosen: subsets, finitely many items, identical objects from one pile.

        • Write the generating function for choices where the exponent records the total value, such as making change with coins of given denominations.

        • Encode conditions on each type of object: upper bounds, lower bounds, finite ranges, even or odd numbers, multiples of a fixed number, unlimited supply.

        • Write generating functions for unordered selections with repetition (combinations with repetition) and integer solutions of with constraints on each variable, using a product of generating functions.
