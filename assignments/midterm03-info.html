    Midterm 3 Information#

    Format and rules#

    Midterm 3 takes place on Friday, November 13, lasts 50 minutes, and has five problems. One problem consists of true-or-false questions; the others ask for computations with a short explanation.

    You may bring one handwritten study sheet or formula sheet on one A4 page, written on both sides. No other notes or outside materials may be used during the exam. You are encouraged to use an offline calculator.

    Show the method that justifies each answer. A number without supporting work may receive only partial credit. When you use Burnside’s lemma, show the cycles or the cycle count of each group element and the sum you divide.

    Coverage#

    The exam covers groups, permutations, group actions, and Burnside’s lemma: the material from Lecture 23 (groups, symmetries of polygons, and permutations) through Burnside’s lemma. It corresponds to the Groups and Group actions and Burnside’s lemma lecture notes and to Homework 8. Pólya counting and symmetries of graphs are not tested. Material from earlier units is not tested directly.

    Practice worksheets#

    These optional worksheets are not collected or graded. Together they cover every kind of problem on the exam, with different data, and each one has posted solutions that model the expected amount of explanation. Work on paper first, then check your work against the solutions.

    • Worksheet 1: Groups and Permutations (solutions): groups, subgroups, Lagrange’s theorem, cyclic groups, cycle notation, composition, inverses, and orders.

    • Worksheet 2: Orbits, Stabilizers, and Fixed Sets (solutions): moving colorings, orbits, stabilizers, fixed sets, and the orbit–stabilizer theorem.

    • Worksheet 3: Rotations of Solids (solutions): the rotations of the tetrahedron, the cube, and a triangular prism as permutations, sorted by their axes.

    • Worksheet 4: Burnside’s Lemma (solutions): flags, necklaces, bracelets, grids, solids, and strings up to symmetry.
