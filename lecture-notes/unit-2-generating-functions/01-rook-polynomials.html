      6. Rook polynomials and forbidden positions#

      An assignment problem may prohibit some person–position pairings. A rook board keeps those prohibited cells visible while we count only assignments that use allowed cells. The coefficients of a rook polynomial count legal partial assignments; the coefficient for all rows counts legal complete assignments.

      After studying these notes, you will be able to do the following.

      • Model allowed assignments by white squares of a board and nonattacking rook placements.

      • Compute rook numbers and the rook polynomial of a small finite board.

      • Use a disjoint-board product and a use-or-omit recurrence to compute a rook polynomial.

      • Use the appropriate rook number to count legal complete assignments.

      • Count injective assignments when there are more positions than objects.

      6.7 What the polynomial has recorded#

      The coefficient records the number of legal injective assignments of rows to distinct columns. In particular, counts the legal assignments that use every row. The rook polynomial is a finite first example of a generating function: one algebraic object stores a whole sequence of related counts.

      Ordinary generating functions extend this bookkeeping idea from the finite sequence to arbitrary sequences indexed by size.
