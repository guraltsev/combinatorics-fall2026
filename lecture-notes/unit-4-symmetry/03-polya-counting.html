      16. Pólya counting#

      Burnside’s lemma in Lecture Note 15 counts the orbits of all colorings of a set of positions. Often we want to prescribe how many positions get each color, as in the following problem.

      Problem 1 (Necklaces with three black and three white beads)#

      How many necklaces have beads, of which exactly are black and are white? How many bracelets?

      Burnside’s lemma still applies, but the computation of each fixed set has to take the numbers of black and white beads into account, and a new problem with different numbers would require a new computation. Pólya’s method records the cycle lengths of every element of the group in a polynomial called the cycle index. Substituting into the cycle index then produces a generating function, in the sense of Unit II, whose coefficients count orbits of colorings with every possible number of positions of each color.

      After studying these notes, you will be able to do the following.

      • Explain why moving a coloring by a group element does not change how many positions have each color.

      • Compute the cycle index of a group acting on a set of positions.

      • Count colorings up to symmetry by substituting into the cycle index.

      • Use Pólya’s theorem to count orbits of colorings with a prescribed number of positions of each color.

      • Distinguish necklace counts from bracelet counts by identifying mirror images.

      Throughout, a group acts on a finite set of positions, and acts on colorings of by carrying colors along with positions, as in Lecture Note 15.
