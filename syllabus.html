Course overview#

University of Arkansas mathematics course listings

Prerequisites: MATH 26103 or MATH 28003
Pre- or Corequisite: MATH 30803 or MATH 30903.

You should be comfortable working with sets, functions, relations, basic proofs and mathematical arguments, algebraic manipulation, and polynomials.

Combinatorics begins with a simple questions: How many ways can we arrange a collection of objects? Can a schedule, pairing, or design be made at all? What changes when two arrangements should count as the same? These questions show up in mathematics, computer science, probability, and more…

We will learn foundational combinatorial techniques, the inclusion-exclusion principle, allowing us to count efficiently.

Next, we will learn generating functions, that will show us how algebra can be used as an efficient bookkeeping combinatorial quantities we become familiar with at the start of the course.

Then we will learn to model systems by describing them using recurrence relations: rules that govern how later states depend on previous ones.

With this background in hand we will focus on additional topics that may include modular arithmetic and applications to cryptography, coding theory, graph theory, counting arrangements with symmetries, and elementary algebraic structures.

Course Contents#

Unit

Main capability

Enumeration and inclusion-exclusion

Define discrete objects precisely and count them using case structure, bijections, double counting, and overlapping restrictions.

Generating-function foundations

Encode sequences as formal power series and interpret algebraic operations as counting operations.

Recurrence models

Select states and initial conditions, derive and solve recurrences, and compare recursive, memoized, and bottom-up evaluation.

Learning Outcomes#

By the end of the course, you should be able to:

  1. Use basic counting techniques, including permutations, combinations with repetition, and careful accounting for overcounting.

  2. Analyze a discrete model by identifying its objects, states, parameters, and constraints.

  3. Choose and switch representations to reveal a model’s useful structure.

  4. Explain a combinatorial argument clearly: describe the model, choose a representation, and apply the right known techniques.

  5. Describe a recursive model and analyze its behavior by identifying state information, a recurrence, and initial conditions, and by finding a closed form when possible.

  6. Use generating functions as formal algebraic models and extract the information they encode.

  7. Work with selected advanced discrete systems, which may include symmetry, graphs, algorithms, and complexity analysis.

Course organization#

In class, I will introduce new topics and talk through their applications with you.

After most meetings, online questions provide a quick review of basic skills; they are graded automatically, and most allow multiple attempts.

I will assign hand-in homework weekly.

We will have three midterms and a final-period exam.

Attendance and participation#

Attendance is not graded, and there is no absence penalty. If you need to miss class, you can use the posted notes, online questions, homework, and other resources to keep up independently.

Please keep track of course material, announcements, deadlines, and assessments. If your online work, homework, or exams suggest that your current study routine is not serving you, I may reach out with ideas such as regular attendance or office hours.

Please plan to be present for scheduled exams. I will not be able to offer make up exams except for excused emergencies.

Weekly hand-in homework#

A typical assignment has several problems and may involve computation, modeling, and proofs or other mathematical arguments. Please submit it online as a PDF (a handwritten, scanned PDF is welcome), make sure it is legible and labels each problem, explain your method as well as your final answer, and write complete mathematical statements. Please follow the deadline listed on the assignment page.

If you need more time, talk to me in advance of the deadline so we can work out an extension. We will deal with unforeseen emergencies on a case by case basis.

Online questions#

Online questions normally follow a lecture and are due by the start of our next class meeting. They focus on comfort with key vocabulary and formulas.

Unit exams#

Every exam problem will be parallel to an assigned homework problem in its required method and main solution steps, though the parameters, objects, or wording may change.
